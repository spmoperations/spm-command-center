The short version
  • Phase 1 connects three things: your Slack, your Telegram and your Fathom call recordings.
  • Once an hour it collects what is new and puts the most important things first.
  • It finds the tasks that are yours and gives each one a due date.
  • You reply or delegate from one page. Your reply goes out under your own name.

How it works

Your messages and calls come in. Once an hour the Command Center sorts them. You answer from one page.

The picture in plain words

  1. Your messages and calls come in. Messages from Slack and Telegram. Calls from Fathom, with the notes and the transcript.
  2. Once an hour, the Command Center collects what is new.
  3. It gets sorted. Simple rules put the urgent messages on top, for example a direct message or a place where you are tagged. Then the tasks that are yours are picked out, each with a due date.
  4. You see it all in one place, in the tabs you already know from your prototype.
  5. You reply or delegate. It is sent right away from your own Slack or Telegram, under your name.

Nothing happens without you. Nothing is sent, and nothing becomes a task, until you click.

Where AI is used, and where it is not

Most of the Command Center runs on plain rules. AI is used for only one job, the one that needs a real understanding of language.

Plain rules, no AI

  • Collecting new messages and calls.
  • Deciding what is urgent. You set the rules: which people, which channels, and anything where you are tagged.
  • Labelling a chat Agency or Consulting.
  • Call notes, transcript and the tasks from a call. These come ready-made from Fathom.
  • Suggesting who can take a task, by matching it with your team list.
  • Sending your replies and delegations.

AI, only when needed

Finding tasks in your messages. Once an hour, and only on new messages that may need something from you.

Why this way: rules give the same result every time, they are quick, and they cost very little to run. It also means less of your message text is ever sent to AI.

If you answer it yourself

  1. Open the message.
  2. Write your reply.
  3. Click Send and mark done. Your reply shows up in Slack under your name, and the message leaves your inbox.

If someone else should do it

  1. Click Delegate.
  2. Pick the person and write a short note.
  3. They get a Slack message from you with the task and your note.
  4. The task now shows under their name. You can take it back at any time.

How often it checks

The Command Center checks for new things once an hour. Each check is its own small program with its own timer, and you can change the timer yourself.

Good to know

  • A new message can take up to an hour to show up.
  • In a hurry? Click Run now and it checks straight away.
  • Your replies are never delayed. They are sent the moment you click. If one fails to send, it tries again by itself.
  • Why not check every second? Slack, Telegram and Fathom only let an app ask so often. Checking once an hour keeps us well inside their limits, so the connection is never blocked.

Adding your team later

Phase 1 is only for you. It is built so more people can be added later, without starting again.

How that will work

  • Each person gets their own login.
  • Each person connects their own Slack, Telegram and calls. It takes a few clicks.
  • Nobody can see anyone else's messages or calls. Yours stay private to you.
  • The team list, the departments and delegated tasks are shared.
  • Delegated tasks land in the other person's own task list once they have a login. Until then, you tick delegated tasks off yourself.

Your idea from the call, linking each person's view to their clients in the Control Room, comes at that stage. It is not part of Phase 1.

  • Reply from the page and see your reply in Slack or Telegram under your name.

Things we still need to check

  • Telegram. Connecting it works like logging in on a new device. We test this first, because it is the part we are least sure about. Telegram also watches accounts that connect through other apps, so we read only the chats you choose, and slowly.
  • Tags in channels you did not choose. We use Slack's search for this and need to test it. If it does not work, we catch tags in the channels you chose and in your direct messages.
  • Slack approval. Your Slack may ask an owner to approve the new app once.
  • Calls recorded by someone else. Fathom only shows us calls you recorded, or calls shared with you. Ask teammates to share the calls they record for you.
  • Client Slacks. If some clients are in a different Slack, each one needs its own connection.
  • AI cost. It should be low, because AI is used for only one job. We will know the real number after one day of use.
